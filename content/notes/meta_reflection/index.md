On my LinkedIn page, under my Meta entry, I have this quote from Carlos Alcaraz, my favorite tennis player:

> "Sometimes I talk to myself about what is most important: if I win or doing great things."

My biggest fear before joining Meta was to forget who I am. And I feel I'm just about to.

It's hard to hear your own voice when all the other ones are deafening: What's SOTA and what's not, what you can and cannot say, whom to worship and whom to look down upon, how your hands are tied, whom to be grateful for and sell your soul to. You listen so carefully, follow so closely, think you're such a good student of this game, only lose everything beautiful about machine learning and life.

Modern tennis rewards "point players," who build points so carefully and mechanically that they become great at winning yet ugly to watch. Model launches at Meta are more or less the same. Shut up and learn, and one day you'll master the game. Point by point, you forget why you play, and why winning ever mattered.

Carlos plays on instinct, passion, and happiness. Few coaches would tell their trainees to follow his footsteps. In his dog days from late 2024 into early 2025, the doubts were so overwhelmingly loud that you'd think he was about to wash out. And what if he had? Being proud of the way you are is the rarest commodity in sports, career, and life. I'll never find out what I love or hear what my instincts say unless I shut out all the external noise. Winning means nothing if I lose myself.
